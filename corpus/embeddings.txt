What is an embedding?

An embedding is a way of turning a piece of text into a list of numbers, called a vector,
such that pieces of text with similar meaning end up close together in that numeric space.
This is different from older methods like TF-IDF or Jaccard similarity, which only compare
the literal words that appear in two documents. An embedding model is trained on huge
amounts of text so it learns relationships between words and concepts, not just their
spelling. As a result, two sentences with almost no shared vocabulary can still land close
together in vector space if they mean the same thing. For example "the deadline was
extended" and "they pushed back the due date" share almost no words, but a good embedding
model will place their vectors close to each other because the meaning overlaps heavily.

Embeddings usually have a fixed length, such as 384 or 1536 numbers per piece of text,
regardless of how long the original text was. Distance between two embeddings is typically
measured with cosine similarity, which looks at the angle between two vectors rather than
their raw magnitude. A cosine similarity close to 1 means the two texts are very similar in
meaning; a value close to 0 means they are unrelated.
