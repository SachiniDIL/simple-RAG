What happens at retrieval time?

Retrieval is the step where a user's query gets turned into an embedding using the exact same
model that was used to embed the documents, and that query vector is then compared against
every stored chunk vector in the vector store. The chunks with the highest similarity scores,
usually the top 3 to 10, are pulled out as the most relevant pieces of text for that query.
This is often called "top-k retrieval," where k is however many chunks you decide to keep.

Retrieval quality depends heavily on how the corpus was chunked and embedded upstream. If a
relevant sentence was chunked awkwardly, split across two chunks, or diluted inside a chunk
covering unrelated content, it may not surface even though the information technically exists
in the corpus. This is why some systems combine retrieval methods: pairing a semantic vector
search with an older lexical method like BM25 (a more refined relative of TF-IDF) so that exact
keyword matches are not lost when the embedding model alone would rank them low. Combining
scores from both methods is usually called hybrid search.
