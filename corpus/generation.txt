What does the "generation" step in RAG actually do?

Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG, combines the retrieval step described elsewhere with a
final step where a language model writes an answer. Instead of asking the language model a
question directly and hoping it already knows the answer from its training data, the retrieved
chunks are inserted into the prompt alongside the user's question, typically with an instruction
telling the model to answer only using the provided material. This is what "augmented" refers to
in the name: the model's own knowledge is augmented with fresh, specific context at the moment
of answering.

This matters for two reasons. First, it lets a language model answer questions about information
it was never trained on, such as a company's internal documents or events after its training
cutoff. Second, it reduces hallucination, because the model has real source text in front of it
to ground its answer in, rather than needing to recall facts purely from memory. A well-built RAG
system will also cite which chunk each part of the answer came from, so a user can verify the
claim against the original source rather than trusting the model blindly.
